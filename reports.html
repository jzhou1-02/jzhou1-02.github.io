Mid-Atlantic Regional

APR 2025 · CAR III
Assigned distance
19.5 m
Actual stop
21.3 m
Error
+1.8 m
Placement
7th / 14

What worked

  • Car drove dead straight — the rear differential fix from winter held
  • EHS review passed first round, zero reviewer comments
  • Calibration workflow finished with 12 minutes to spare
  • Voltage stayed stable across both runs — Zn/Cu stack was never the problem

What broke

  • Stopped 1.8 m long on both runs, consistent direction — systematic, not random
  • Venue gym was ~8 °C colder than our lab; iodine clock ran slow
  • Our calibration curve had no temperature term at all
  • No thermometer in the kit, so we couldn't even quantify it on-site
Carry forwardCalibrate at venue temperature, or build the temperature correction into the curve. A thermometer is now permanently in the competition kit.

Home track trials

NOV 2024 · CAR II
Assigned distance
15.0 m
Actual stop
14.1 m
Error
−0.9 m
Placement
trial run

What worked

  • First successful reaction-timed stop — the iodine clock concept is proven
  • Photoresistor circuit triggered cleanly on the color change
  • Calibration curve was linear across the tested concentration range

What broke

  • Undershoot traced to wheel slip on the gym floor at launch
  • Reagent mixing procedure varied by operator — needed a written protocol
  • One run lost entirely to a loose relay wire
Carry forwardWrite the mixing protocol down and assign one calibrator per event. Distance error has two parts — reaction timing AND drivetrain repeatability — and they must be characterized separately.

First outing (the pole-hit of legend)

APR 2023 · CAR I
Assigned distance
17.0 m
Actual stop
a pole
Error
n/a
Placement
DNF

What worked

  • The car moved, which at the time felt like a miracle
  • Team morale, somehow

What broke

  • Pressure propulsion was a different car every run — repeatability near zero
  • Mechanical timer drifted with spring tension
  • Steering had no trim adjustment; it veered left and met the pole
Carry forwardRepeatability beats power. Everything since — galvanic cells, reaction-timed stops, calibration culture — descends from this one bad afternoon.
